# HRM body segmentation

This **binary semantic segmentation** project uses RGB images captured against a black background
and masks generated by the existing image-processing pipeline. The default model is an
ImageNet-pretrained **U-Net / ResNet18**. A 384-pixel input, close to the original resolution,
a small decoder, and CUDA mixed precision balance speed and boundary accuracy.
Use `benchmark` on the target GPU to assess real-time performance.

## 2026-10-24 update: base/tip-oriented perspective augmentation and paper assets

Start with `runs/perspective_pose_followup_20261024/report.html` for the follow-up results.
The V1/V2 models, original data, and RGB-D evaluation results remain unchanged.

- `feasibility_augmentation_v3.py` uses pinhole projection of a virtual plane aligned with the original
  base-to-tip direction. Base-near and tip-near views are generated at 25°/45°/60° and combined with
  background, position, rotation, scale, and three-gap occlusion changes. These angles describe the
  **augmentation plane tilt**, not actual HRM joint angles or camera poses. This does not generate
  new 3D observations or depth for a curved cylinder.
- The same homography transforms RGB and visible masks. A medial skeleton recomputed after perspective
  transformation can produce spurious branches at the wider end, so occluders are placed using the
  original skeleton transformed by the same homography. This reference is used only for label/occluder
  generation; the network receives RGB only.
- `run_perspective_v3.py` retains V2's saved 1,600 training images, 96 validation images, 16 epochs,
  seed 173, optimizer, and three validation conditions. V3 is trained separately with the same ImageNet
  initialization procedure, not by fine-tuning V2. This is a single-seed development experiment,
  not an independent blind test.
- RGB, visible pseudo-masks, occluder masks, and prediction PNGs from three methods for 128 source images
  × 11 conditions are preserved in `segmentation_evaluation/assets/<condition>/<source>/`. Per-case
  `provenance.json` and the aggregate `cases.json` record source paths/SHA256, timestamps, seeds, H,
  base/tip magnification ratios, and depth incompatibility. Relative image paths in `figure_sources.json`
  are resolved against `asset_path_base`.
- All 4,224 comparisons completed without processing exceptions. V3 Dice was 97.78–98.34% for perspective
  alone and 96.18–96.52% for background/placement/three-gap combinations. The maximum improvement over V2
  in perspective conditions was modest, approximately 0.44 percentage points; clean performance decreased
  from 98.09% to 97.93%, approximately 0.16 percentage points. OpenCV achieved higher pseudo-label Dice
  with perspective-only changes; learned models were stronger in combined background/occlusion conditions.
  Synthesis retaining the original background can leave inpainting artifacts and is not a physical
  recapture. All 54,912 metrics independently recomputed from saved PNGs matched the recorded values.
  All 122 regression tests passed.
- **Original depth is never paired with perspective-warped RGB.** The new perspective experiments evaluate
  segmentation only. `paper_pose_summary.html/csv/json` separately reaggregate the existing
  **V2 synthetic-occlusion results using original RGB-D**. `paper_pose_source_index.json` links original
  RGB/depth, cropped camera intrinsics K, capture metadata, and shape NPZ paths.

For three gaps totaling 30% in the paper occlusion assets, the mean FK tip-position change relative
to the unoccluded estimate was 0.413 mm for straight poses and 0.554 mm for bent poses; 95.0% and
90.6% of changes, respectively, were at most 1 mm. This is **neither accuracy against physical ground
truth nor a claim that every frame is within 1 mm**. Across all 624 occluded frame-condition pairs,
the maximum position changes were 2.280 mm for straight poses and 3.716 mm for bent poses.
Joint angles are reported separately as the mean per-frame wrapped 18-joint RMSE, and orientation
as the SO(3) rotation difference. These experiments exclude depth at known synthetic occluder locations;
rejection of valid depth from real occluders and independent marker/manual ground-truth validation
still require separate experiments.

## 2026-10-24 V2: multiple-gap/perspective augmentation and angle/FK sensitivity

The second experiment is documented in the **[V2 report](runs/occlusion_pose_20261024/report.html)**.
The first pilot results remain intact. Eighty RGB-D pairs were captured against a new black background;
the motors were not operated.

- Training: ResNet18 U-Net, 1,600 images from the two existing training sequences, 16 epochs.
  Checkpoints were selected using 96 validation images × 3 conditions.
- Augmentation: one thin gap (approximately 2%), one thick gap (10/20/30%), three separated gaps
  (15/30% total); black/gray/white/colored opaque objects; background replacement; bright distractors;
  rotation/scale/translation/planar homography.
- Segmentation: OpenCV/V1/V2 were compared on the same 128 frames × 10 conditions. Dice was
  20.33% / 84.49% / 96.03% for combined background/perspective/three-gap conditions and
  31.69% / 95.62% / 96.50% for three gaps totaling 30% alone. V2 was approximately 0.06/0.10
  percentage points below V1 for single 20/30% gaps, so it did not improve every condition.
- RGB-D: 7 conditions × 3 methods were evaluated on 40 new frames and 64 archived bent-pose frames.
  Changes in 18 joint angles and FK tip position/orientation are computed **relative to each method's
  unoccluded result for the same frame**, not physical ground-truth accuracy. Archived RGB/depth file
  indices are misaligned, so pairs were matched using `source_time_ns`.
- The development test sequence was already examined in the first pilot; it is not a new blind test.
  Masks are OpenCV pseudo-labels, and the new camera images have no manually annotated ground truth.

RGB-D experiments apply occlusion without coordinate warping and exclude depth at known synthetic
occluder locations. Predictions are not clipped using ground-truth masks, and original depth at hidden
locations is not reused. This is a **missing-depth simulation**, not automatic rejection of valid depth
from real occluding objects. Perspective-warped RGB is used only for segmentation and never paired
with original depth. A homography changes planar appearance; it does not create a genuine new
3D viewpoint or wide-angle lens distortion.

The new offline shape front end orders Lee-skeleton paths from multiple visible fragments starting
at the lower base. It parameterizes observations by cumulative 3D chord length and therefore does not
know the true arc length across gaps. Origin-constrained quartic fitting, length normalization, and
19-direction/18-joint DH projection are reused directly from production `postprocess.py` via its AST.
FK agreement was checked by separately compiling actual function bodies from `surgical_tool.cpp`.
Temporal Kalman filtering and initial base averaging are disabled; each frame is processed independently.
This offline experimental front end omits the existing 2D extrapolation and does not replace the
entire production pipeline.

The initial front end discarded skeleton fragments shorter than 8 pixels, sometimes missing a short
base fragment despite a correct mask. Separate results with `--minimum-path-points 1` preserve even
singleton observations. The default of 8 and the initial results remain available. This was a
development-stage change after diagnosing evaluation failures, not blind validation.

With the final short-fragment handling, V2 produced angles/FK for all 104 frames × 7 conditions
(this does not mean 100% accuracy). For three gaps totaling 30%, **changes relative to its own clean
estimate** on the new black background averaged 1.265° q18 RMSE, 0.413 mm FK position, and 3.305° FK
rotation; the archived bent sequence yielded 1.477° / 0.554 mm / 3.603°. Joint RMSE is computed across
18 joint differences per frame and then averaged. The 95th-percentile FK-position changes were
0.804/1.390 mm, respectively. Valid V2 comparisons for this condition increased from 15/40 and 31/64
with min8 to 40/40 and 64/64 with min1. High mask overlap does not eliminate angle/pose differences,
and tip position alone cannot establish body-shape accuracy. All 90 code regression tests passed.
Real-occluder valid-depth contamination and independent ground-truth validation require further experiments.

A basic-augmentation U-Net and a background/placement/lighting/occlusion-augmentation U-Net were
compared using 1,200 source training images sampled uniformly from two capture sequences, 12 epochs

MobileSAM selected the background rather than the body when given only the full crop box (0% Dice
in middle-occlusion conditions). A hybrid using boxes/points from U-Net predictions was also compared;
ground-truth prompts were not used. On the RTX 3060, U-Net alone took approximately **5 ms** and the
hybrid approximately **49 ms**. These inference timings exclude camera reception, ROS, 3D computation,
and display, and do not represent full-system FPS. MobileSAM used pretrained weights for inference
only; neither separate SAM training nor YOLO training was performed.

Twenty new RGB-D pairs were captured using the connected D405, and 300 inferences were completed
under clean and synthetic 10/20% occlusion conditions. These were qualitative checks, not images with
physical occluders. Augmented U-Net retained both body fragments but sometimes misclassified bright
nearby objects; in some hybrid results, those false positives expanded into the background.
Real-occlusion capture, manual mask review, and joint-angle/tip-error validation are required before
real-time deployment. Background depth caused by nearby-object false positives in clean images was
also preserved in the results. Within-mask median filtering alone cannot fix this misclassification.
**All 54 regression tests passing** verifies code behavior, not model accuracy or successful recovery
of the true 3D shape.

## Dataset observations

- `datasets/image_rgb` / `datasets/image_segmentation`: **5,389 pairs** with matching filenames.
- RGB: `337×379×3`, uint8. Masks: `337×379`, uint8, containing **only 0 and 255**.
- White mask pixels represent the **entire white body**. Black base/tip attachments are excluded,
  and individual joints are not labeled.
- The stored `0/255` format is intentional. Immediately before training, `(mask > 0)` converts masks
  to **float32 0/1**. The model outputs sigmoid probabilities, binarized at 0.5 by default;
  output PNGs are saved as `0/255` again.
- Foreground occupies approximately 3.1–5.9% of each image. The loss is `0.5 BCE + 0.5 Dice`.

Capture sessions are currently separated by gaps exceeding 2 seconds in the seconds/nanoseconds
timestamps embedded in filenames. By default, 3,860 images from two sessions are used for training
and 1,529 images from the last session for validation. Adjacent frames are not randomly mixed.
**There is no independent real-surgical-environment test set yet.** Validation selects checkpoints,
so validation results must not be interpreted as independent test performance.
Images from nested source/legacy folders are not added automatically.

## Workflow

```bash
source .venv/bin/activate

# Check all masks and split by capture session. Original images remain unchanged.
python hrm.py prepare

# Obtain 300 backgrounds from official public surgical videos, retaining the initial 30.
python backgrounds.py --train 180 --val 60 --test 60

# Preview random placement alone and placement combined with appearance degradation.
python hrm.py preview --mode placement --output runs/placement_random_preview.jpg
python hrm.py preview --mode combined --output runs/combined_random_preview.jpg

# Default: 50 originals x 2 modes x 3 variants = 300 saved RGB/mask pairs.
python hrm.py export-augmented

# RTX 4090 example. On a 3060, start with batch-size 8 and reduce to 4 if needed.
python hrm.py train --epochs 20 --batch-size 16 --size 384 --output runs/hrm_random_placement

# After training, evaluate each condition separately on the same validation session.
python hrm.py evaluate --checkpoint runs/hrm_random_placement/best.pt \
  --output runs/hrm_random_placement/evaluation.json

# Input may be an image, image directory, video, or webcam index.
python hrm.py predict --checkpoint runs/hrm_random_placement/best.pt --source datasets/image_rgb
python hrm.py predict --checkpoint runs/hrm_random_placement/best.pt --source 0 --show
```

`prepare` does not overwrite an existing `datasets/split.json`. Use `--overwrite` only when deliberately
changing the split. Training filenames are stored in checkpoints to check evaluation overlap with
training data. An independent capture session can be reserved for testing, for example with
`--val-group session_03 --test-group session_02`, but this leaves only one training session
with the current three-session dataset.

`train` does not overwrite existing runs. Choose a different `--output` for repeat experiments.
`best.pt` maximizes the average of clean-validation Dice and Dice on 256 fixed synthetic validation
images. `last.pt`, `history.csv`, `config.json`, and copies of split/background provenance are also
saved. HRM placement, orientation, and appearance are augmented on the fly, so tens of thousands
of synthetic images need not be stored in advance.

`export-augmented` saves inspection RGB images and `0/255` masks to `image_rgb/` and
`image_segmentation/` under `datasets/augmented_random`, together with `manifest.json`, copies of
background provenance/licenses, and `preview.jpg`. Customize with `--count`, `--variants`, `--modes`,
`--split`, and `--output`. Existing output directories are not overwritten; specify a new `--output`
when exporting again. These are seed-recorded augmentation examples, not reconstructed training batches.
The 60 pairs in `datasets/augmented_samples` remain as examples of the earlier 30-background,
restricted-pose setup. See the [300-pair preview](datasets/augmented_random/preview.jpg) and
[original / placement-augmented / target comparison](runs/placement_preview.jpg).

The pretrained encoder is downloaded at the first training run. `--no-pretrained` is intended for
offline functionality checks.

For an accuracy comparison, try `--encoder resnet34 --size 512`. Improvement is not guaranteed;
measure VRAM usage and processing time as well. The default U-Net uses the established
[segmentation-models-pytorch implementation](https://smp.readthedocs.io/en/latest/models.html).
Current labels are whole-body pixel masks and are not converted into YOLO object polygons.

## Backgrounds and augmentation

`backgrounds.py` has obtained **300 images from 201 source videos** in the
[official Endoscapes dataset](https://github.com/CAMMA-public/Endoscapes): 180 images/120 videos
for training, 60 images/41 videos for validation, and 60 images/40 videos for testing, preserving the
official video-level split. The original 30 images are retained; new videos are prioritized before
adding temporally separated frames from the same video. All videos depict laparoscopic
cholecystectomy, not a range of surgical procedures or organ regions.
HTTP Range requests retrieve only the archive listing and selected JPEGs, not the entire large ZIP.
If Range requests are unsupported, retrieval fails rather than downloading the full archive.
Existing files are checked against saved SHA256 values and verified to decode and contain nonblack
content, then skipped for download. `--train`, `--val`, and `--test` specify minimum target counts;
smaller values do not delete existing files. Metadata from before expansion is preserved in
`datasets/backgrounds/manifests/`. The [background preview](datasets/backgrounds/preview.jpg)
shows representative frames from 60 videos.

You may add your own backgrounds to `datasets/backgrounds/{train,val,test}`. Do not distribute
frames from the same background video across multiple splits. Record the source and usage terms
of added backgrounds separately. If a split has no backgrounds, a message is printed and generic
random colors/textures are used. These fallback backgrounds are not surgical images.

The original Endoscapes data and derived datasets are subject to **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. Noncommercial
use, attribution, and share-alike requirements apply separately from this repository's code license.
See `datasets/backgrounds/sources.json` and the license files for exact sources, licenses, and citations.

| Augmentation | Implementation and target handling |
|---|---|
| Background compositing | Preserve RGB inside the body mask and replace the background; feather only inside the boundary |
| Placement/orientation | Extract the body, place it randomly, and rotate 0–360°. Limit requested scales of 0.65–1.35 to fit the rotated image. Apply the same coordinates to the mask |
| Body color | RGB scaling while retaining surface shading |
| Lighting | Gamma, brightness, color temperature, spatial shadows, and bright reflection regions |
| Smoke | Nonuniform translucent smoke layers; retain the object target mask |
| Occlusion | Opaque irregular regions; remove occluded pixels from targets as well |
| Camera | Gaussian blur, sensor noise, and JPEG degradation |
| Background-only scenes | Approximately 5% of training; all-zero targets suppress false positives |

`placement` changes only body position, rotation, scale, and background. Approximately 70% of
placements retain the entire body; approximately 30% attempt partial clipping at an image edge.
Even clipped cases retain **at least 60%** of transformed foreground area before subsequent opaque
occlusion. Masks use nearest-neighbor warping; RGB is interpolated after multiplication by the mask
to avoid mixing the original black background into the boundary. `combined` adds color, lighting,
smoke, occlusion, and camera degradation to this placement.

Training mixes random placement, mild conditions, combined conditions, and some original appearances.
Inspect individual modes with `preview --mode placement`, `--mode combined`, `--mode smoke`,
`--mode occlusion`, and related options. Validation augmentation is fixed by filename and seed
and never uses training backgrounds.

Smoke and occlusion are approximations for preliminary robustness experiments, not physical
reproductions of surgical smoke, lighting, or tissue contact. The 300 backgrounds sample one surgical
procedure; synthesis alone cannot establish real-surgical-environment performance. 2D augmentation
does not create new joint shapes or 3D camera viewpoints. Additional capture and validation in the
target environment are needed. Review representative masks because image-processing label boundary
errors can also be learned by the model.

## Evaluation and speed measurement

```bash
python hrm.py evaluate --checkpoint runs/hrm_random_placement/best.pt --limit 300 \
  --output runs/hrm_random_placement/evaluation_quick.json
python hrm.py benchmark --checkpoint runs/hrm_random_placement/best.pt \
  --source datasets/image_rgb/00000001_1790484638_813745361.png
python -m unittest -v test_hrm.py
```

Evaluation records Dice, IoU, precision/recall, and boundary F1 with a 2-pixel tolerance at the input
resolution. For `negative` conditions with no target foreground, inspect `predicted_foreground_fraction`
and `false_positive_pixels_per_image` rather than Dice. Evaluation and benchmarking automatically
use the checkpoint's input size.

Benchmarks use batch size 1 after warmup and include CUDA synchronization. `model` measures model
execution on a tensor already on the GPU. `pipeline` includes CPU preprocessing, CPU/GPU transfer,
model execution, original-resolution restoration, and binarization. Camera reception, video decoding,
overlays, display, and file saving are excluded.
**Do not interpret measurements from a 4090 as FPS on a 3060/5060 Ti.**

Image inference saves original-resolution `*_mask.png` and `*_overlay.jpg` files.
Video/webcam inference saves `overlay.mp4`; `--save-masks` also saves per-frame prediction-mask PNGs.
Omit `--show` on headless servers.
